Who it reaches

Coaches practise the tools
before passing them on.

First for themselves. Then for the coaches around them, then players, then the parents and caregivers in a player’s corner. The more of that circle speaks the same language, the more sound the player’s surround sound becomes.

The coach

Does the work first, as a participant. Certification comes from doing the Life Plan themselves.

Other coaches

Catalysts run it with peers. Coaches who can lead a session without us.

Players

One lesson on court, one off court. Tools a player can use between points.

Parents & caregivers

Some coaches set a standard: complete the 45-minute basics before we work together.

Method

Experience it. Rehearse it.
Then review it.

Every tool goes through the same three moves before a coach uses it with anyone else. The full ten-pillar framework is in the appendix.

Experience

The coach tries the exercise first, as a participant.

Rehearse

Then uses it inside a tennis-specific scenario.

Review

Then decides how, and whether, it fits their own coaching.

One tool, as an example
“Long exhale. Next point.”
A reset a coach practises on themselves first, between drills or after a call they disagree with. Then teaches the player to use it between points. Simple enough to hold under pressure, which is the whole point.
